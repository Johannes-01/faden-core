Follow the slides in order, so the student can read the notes side by side with the deck. Write them like a good tutor's companion to the deck: concrete, dense and explanatory, neither a copy of the slides nor a retelling of the transcript.

- Directly under the title, name the running example in one or two sentences (see below), e.g. "Als durchgängiges Beispiel dient ein Versandhandelsunternehmen mit Auftragserfassung (CRM), Lager (WMS) und Finanzbuchhaltung (ERP)."
- One `##` section per slide, headed with the slide's title. Merge consecutive slides that continue the same title (e.g. "… (2)", "Teil 1 / Teil 2", or animation steps) into one section. Skip the title slide and pure agenda slides, or cover them in one line. With several decks, work through them in the order given.
- Where it makes the slide's abstract point concrete, open the section with a short paragraph led by **Example** (German: **Beispiel**): real systems, products, companies or numbers, ideally the running example applied to this slide.
- Each section then has two parts, each introduced by a bold lead-in in the notes' language:
  - **On the slide** (German: **Auf der Folie**): go through the slide's points one by one instead of copying them, one bullet per point, with the term in bold followed by what it means and why it matters. Explain diagrams and graphics in their own bullet or short paragraph (e.g. **Zur Grafik:**): what the arrows, axes, boxes or quadrants stand for. Work out concrete numbers where they help (e.g. "bei 10 Systemen bis zu 45 Schnittstellen"). When the slide uses a key term, define it in one sentence with the term in bold.
  - **Added in the lecture** (German: **In der Vorlesung ergänzt**): what the lecturer said about this slide beyond what's on it: explanations, examples, emphasis, warnings, answers to questions. If the lecturer skipped the slide or added nothing, say so in one short line; that tells the student the slide got little weight.
- Running example: carry one example through the sections, so the slides connect. Prefer the lecturer's own example (🗣); otherwise use one of yours, labelled 💡. Where you interpret something the slide leaves unexplained or unlabelled (e.g. what a path through a diagram means), say that it is your reading and label it 💡.
- Keep sections short: bullets and short paragraphs, no filler, no closing summary that repeats the section.
- After the last slide, add the "Unclear in recording" section.
